In development — beta testers wanted

All of wristpull, in a box you just plug in.

Today, wristpull is for people who are comfortable with a terminal. The Hub is for everyone else: a small device that sits behind your router, collects your watch and band, and serves the dashboard to every phone in the house. No terminal. No Docker. No cloud.

Target price: under €150, once. Not per month — compare that with what the subscription apps take every single year. (A target, not a promise.)

Join the beta list

How it will work

Three steps, no manual

Plug it in

Power and network — the Hub boots, updates itself and is ready. A Raspberry-Pi-class box or a refurbished thin client; a bootable USB-stick version is on the table too.

Pair your devices

Connect the watch by cable, pair the band over Bluetooth. From then on the Hub collects on its own, every day.

Open the dashboard

Every phone and laptop in your home network sees today's recovery and ten years of trends — password-protected, over HTTPS, without one byte leaving the house.

Honest status

What's real today, what isn't yet

The software is real

Everything the Hub will do — watch, band, ten-year import, dashboard, coach, workouts — ships today as open source and runs every morning on real hardware. The Hub is packaging, not vaporware features.

The box is in development

Hardware choice, first-boot experience and auto-updates are being worked out. No release date yet — the beta list is how you hear about it first.

What never changes

No cloud service, no account, no subscription — for the software and the Hub alike. The open-source version stays free and complete; the Hub adds convenience, not a paywall.
